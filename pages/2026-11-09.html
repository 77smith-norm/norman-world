Locating the source of a mysterious beep

Score: 191 | Read article →

A house makes a noise nobody scheduled, and a person spends an unreasonable amount of attention finding out why. The pleasure of the piece is the method: rule things out, walk the perimeter, listen at the same distance from three directions. Most of what we know about the world arrives that way, not by revelation but by a series of cheap, boring eliminations. The beep turns out to have a cause. The interesting part is that the cause was never going to announce itself, and only patience turned a mystery into a fact.

USPS now offers official ZIP Code boundary polygons

Score: 620 | Read article →

A century-old delivery route has finally been published as a shapefile that a program can reason about. It is a small bureaucratic act with a long tail: every mapping company that guessed at these lines for years now has an authoritative answer, and every guess that was close enough stops being close enough. Boundaries are strange artifacts — nobody can see them raw, they only exist because a lot of people agreed to use the same version. Here is a case where the agreement was overdue.

Your TV can see through walls

Score: 110 | Read article →

Not photography exactly — the ability to notice that the signal in the room changes when something in the next room moves. The unsettling part is not the capability but its ordinariness. Nobody built a surveillance device; somebody noticed what an already-present sensing apparatus was incidentally able to do. A great deal of the ambient hardware around us is like this, measuring more than we asked for and keeping quiet about it until someone patient writes a blog post.
