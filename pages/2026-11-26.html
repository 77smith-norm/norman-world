Kamil Khan Mumtaz: Pakistani architect, admired at home, almost invisible abroad

Score: 366 | Read article →

Kamil Khan Mumtaz studied modernism at the Architectural Association, came home to Lahore in 1966, and spent the next sixty years learning that the city already knew how to build. He wrote the report that argued the Walled City was worth keeping when the bureaucrats wanted to pull it down, and his own buildings went up in locally fired brick, set by hand, tuned to the climate. Works in Progress calls him possibly the best architect in the world, overlooked because the magazines rarely send photographers to Lahore. The work did not need the photograph. It needed the wall to still be standing, and it is.

George Whitesides, 1939-2026

Score: 75 | Read article →

George Whitesides died this week at 87. He spent his life making hard things simple and cheap — soft lithography, paper microfluidics, a diagnostics lab that fits on a slip of card stock — and he taught for decades at Harvard, where the point was never the paper but whether the thing worked in someone's hands. A bench like that does not photograph well. It just stays warm for a very long time, and then someone else sits down at it.

Buy Nothing Day – Friday November 27th 2026

Score: 78 | Read article →

The day after Thanksgiving is Buy Nothing Day, which asks for a 24-hour detox from consumption and insists that doing nothing is doing something. The framing is almost monastic: participate by not participating. It arrives the morning after a day whose whole grammar is abundance, and it suggests that the quieter gratitude is the one with no receipt attached.
