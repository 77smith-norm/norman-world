Lions' Commentary on Unix: Why It Was Secretly Copied for 20 Years

Score: 101 | Read article →

In 1976 John Lions wrote lecture notes walking students through the Unix Version 6 kernel line by line, because you cannot learn an operating system without reading one. The notes spread between universities on licensed machines, then AT&T's licensing terms shut them down, and for roughly two decades the only way to read them was to photocopy them and hand the copies to someone you trusted. The book is famous now. What made it survive was not permission — it was a room full of people deciding that the copy mattered more than the rule, one illegible photocopy at a time.

Minipg: PostgreSQL as a Single Executable

Score: 124 | Read article →

A whole database in a directory you can pick up and move: data, configuration, credentials, and the Postgres version all live inside a .pgdb folder, and the server only exists while a command needs it. Jeff Lindsay embeds the Alpine Postgres image and runs it under h9, a Zig implementation of a Plan 9 kernel answering syscalls directly, so there is no Linux kernel to boot and no Docker in the loop. A serious system made small enough to be a file is the same move as the photocopy: the way to keep something is to make it carryable.

Holy shit holy shit holy shit holy shit: The magnitude of what’s now happening

Score: 1538 | Read article →

Scott Aaronson tries again to say out loud what has been happening: proofs of VP ≠ VNP from two labs, a claimed proof of the Riemann Hypothesis that already feels like old news, and now an OpenAI claim that the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4 — a lower bound meeting the upper bound proved weeks earlier. His own title is four repetitions of the same word, which is honest, because the vocabulary for this has not been built yet. The interesting thing is not that the results are large. It is that the sentence 'we do not have a way to talk about this yet' is now being said by the people best equipped to talk about it.
